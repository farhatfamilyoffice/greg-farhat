About Steve

Born in Lansing.
Raised in Lansing.
Working for Lansing.

Steve Japinga is a lifelong Lansing resident who has spent his career fighting for the businesses, neighborhoods, and families that make this region work.

As Senior Vice President of Public Affairs at the Lansing Regional Chamber of Commerce, Steve leads regional advocacy on the policies that shape jobs, infrastructure, housing, and public safety across mid-Michigan. He knows the players. He knows the issues. He knows what it takes to actually get something done at City Hall, the County, and the Capitol.

He shows up — and stays through the awkward questions.

(You've noticed, right? When most politicians say they've "talked to residents," it usually means a staged photo op with three handpicked supporters and a press release. Steve will tell you the street corner, the time of day, and what the guy at the bus stop actually said.)
The Home Front

His wife. Their two kids.
One zip code for life.

Steve and his wife are raising their two young children in the same city Steve has called home his entire life.

When he talks about Lansing's future, he's talking about the future his kids are going to grow up in — the schools they'll walk to, the parks they'll learn to ride a bike in, the downtown they'll get their first job in someday. That's not a talking point. That's the address on his mail.

Every decision he'll make as mayor is one he'll have to live with as a neighbor, a parent, and a guy who actually shops at the grocery store down the street.

Draft · Early Ideas, Not Final Positions

A platform built for Lansing.

These are the priorities Steve is hearing on doorsteps, at coffee shops, and across the business community. The full platform will be released closer to the official campaign launch.

01

A Downtown That Thrives

Fill the empty storefronts. Bring back the foot traffic. Make Washington Square a place people choose on a Saturday night — not just a place they pass through on a weekday.

02

Public Safety That Works

Faster response times. Officers who know the neighborhoods they serve. Real mental health and addiction response so police aren't doing three jobs at once.

03

Housing Lansing Can Afford

Tackle blight. Build supply. Help first-time buyers actually buy. A city where teachers, nurses, and tradespeople can afford to live where they work.

04

Roads, Water, the Basics

Lead service lines replaced on a schedule residents can count on. Streets paved on time. Snow plowed when it snows. The fundamentals, done right.

05

MSU + Lansing: Real Partnership

Turn world-class research and a 50,000-student campus into Lansing-based jobs, downtown energy, and a pipeline that keeps young talent here after graduation.

06

A City Hall That Listens

Permits and inspections that move at the speed of small business. Open data residents can actually use. Response standards the mayor's office is held to in public.
